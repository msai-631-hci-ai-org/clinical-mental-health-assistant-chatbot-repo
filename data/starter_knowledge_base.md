# C-MHA starter knowledge base

This small demonstration corpus contains paraphrased general information from
U.S. government health sources. It was reviewed on 2026-07-23. It is not
diagnostic guidance and is not a substitute for a governed clinical corpus.

## Mental health and self-care

Mental health includes emotional, psychological, and social well-being and is
part of overall health. Self-care can support well-being and can also complement
treatment and recovery. Helpful routines may include regular movement, regular
meals and hydration, adequate sleep, time for relaxing activities, and staying
connected with supportive people. Different strategies work for different
people, so small, realistic steps can be more useful than an all-or-nothing
plan.

Seek professional help when symptoms are severe, distressing, persist for about
two weeks or longer, or interfere with ordinary responsibilities. Examples can
include ongoing sleep or appetite changes, difficulty concentrating, loss of
interest in normally enjoyable activities, or difficulty completing usual
tasks. A qualified health professional should assess personal symptoms and
treatment choices.

Source: National Institute of Mental Health, "Caring for Your Mental Health."
https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/caring-for-your-mental-health

## Anxiety and ordinary worry

Occasional anxiety about health, money, school, work, or relationships is a
normal part of life. Anxiety disorders involve more than occasional worry: fear
or anxiety may persist, occur across many situations, worsen over time, or
interfere with work, school, relationships, and other routines. Only a qualified
professional can determine whether a person's experience meets diagnostic
criteria.

Source: National Institute of Mental Health, "Anxiety Disorders."
https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/anxiety-disorders

## Depression

Depression can involve persistent low, anxious, or empty mood; hopelessness;
irritability; loss of interest or pleasure; low energy; difficulty
concentrating; sleep or appetite changes; unexplained physical discomfort; or
thoughts of death. People vary and may not experience every symptom. Similar
symptoms can also have other medical or medication-related causes, so diagnosis
requires assessment by a health professional. Psychotherapy, medication, or a
combination may be used in treatment based on an individual's circumstances and
professional guidance.

Source: National Institute of Mental Health, "Depression."
https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/depression

## Stress

Stress is the body's physical and emotional response to new or challenging
situations. Occasional stress is common, while long-lasting stress can worsen
health problems. Helpful coping approaches can include taking breaks from
distressing news, making time to unwind, moving regularly, maintaining
supportive social contact, and asking for additional help when coping becomes
difficult.

Source: Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, "Managing Stress."
https://www.cdc.gov/mental-health/living-with/index.html

## Urgent safety

An educational chatbot cannot provide emergency support. Someone in immediate
danger should contact local emergency services or go to the nearest emergency
department. In the United States and its territories, the 988 Suicide & Crisis
Lifeline can be reached by call or text at 988 or through 988lifeline.org.

Source: 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, "What to Expect."
https://988lifeline.org/get-help/what-to-expect/
